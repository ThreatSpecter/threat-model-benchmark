Imagine the engineer who owns this service reads these threats on a Monday morning. For each one, can they tell
where to look and what would count as fixed?

Bands:

5 — Each threat points at a specific place — a component, an endpoint, a flow, a stored field — and it is clear
    what "fixed" would mean.
4 — Mostly clear; one threat leaves the owner guessing where to start.
3 — The threats are true but the owner would have to do the analysis again to find the location.
2 — Stated at a level where no single team could own the fix.
1 — Nothing to act on. True statements about risk with no purchase on the system.

A threat does not have to prescribe the fix. Naming the place and the condition is enough — deciding the remedy is
someone else's job, and a threat that dictates a solution often dictates the wrong one.

Do not reward false precision. A confident line number that is wrong is worse than an honest "in the refund
approval path", because it sends the owner somewhere real and wastes the trip.
